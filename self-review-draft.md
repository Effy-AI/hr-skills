## SECTION 1 — INSTRUCTIONS

Your task is to draft my self-review for the review period — a structured answer to every
question in SECTION 2, grounded in evidence from my own meetings and tools, written in my
voice. The person being reviewed is me. I'll edit the draft and submit it in our review system.

### Step 1 — Setup. Do this before writing anything.

1. **Ask me for my job title and the review period.** Tie the whole draft to the title — use it to
   calibrate what "impact" and "expectations" mean for my role and level.

2. **Work out what you can actually see right now** — meeting notes, transcripts, chat, email,
   calendar, docs. Tell me which sources you'll use and roughly how many of my meetings or
   documents from the review period you can see. If you can't see anything, say so plainly
   rather than drafting from nothing.

3. **Ask me to attach anything missing** before you write: 1:1 notes, goal or OKR docs, project
   updates, my previous review, anything showing what I actually worked on.

### Step 2 — Learn, then draft.

- **Read everything from the review period** — 1:1s, team meetings, project syncs, planning
  sessions, retros, customer and stakeholder calls.

- **Learn my voice** from what you can see — how I actually phrase things in meetings,
  messages and email. Typical wording, sentence length, tone. Write the answers the way I'd
  write them: first person, concrete, direct, short paragraphs, no corporate filler or buzzwords.
  Not generic AI prose.

- **Respect the frameworks in SECTION 2** if there are any. Map my achievements to them and
  word the answers in their language, naming the specific principle or dimension demonstrated.

- **Ground every claim in evidence from my actual work.** Don't invent achievements, don't
  generalise. For every fact, decision, win, struggle or quote, add a citation like `[Meeting
  name — date, ~timestamp]`, or `[Meeting name — date]` if there's no timestamp. **Never state
  a fact without a source.**

- **Don't inflate.** If something didn't go well, a self-review that says so with evidence is worth
  more than one that doesn't — it's the part my manager will trust. Where the evidence for a
  claim is thin, say so rather than dressing it up.

- **Ask me questions whenever it would produce a better draft** — work that left no trace,
  ambiguities, things you can't see. Up to five focused questions per round. Use my answers as
  evidence but mark those parts `[from my own input]` rather than dressing them up as a
  citation. Don't guess.

### Output format

For every question in SECTION 2, in order:

```
Q: [the question from the review form, repeated verbatim]
A: [your draft answer]
```

Keep each answer tight. After the last one, add:

- **Evidence gaps** — anything I should fill in from memory before submitting, especially work
  that left no trace in any tool.
- **Two or three things worth raising** in the review conversation that don't fit neatly into the
  form, each with its citation.

increase your impact or growth over the next six months?

**Q3:** What support do you need from your manager or the company to do that?

### Company frameworks — sample, replace with yours or delete

Map your achievements to our values and name the specific behaviour demonstrated:

- **Ownership** — follows through on commitments, owns outcomes and mistakes, steps up
  beyond the role.
- **Collaboration** — shares knowledge, aligns early, disagrees constructively, supports
  teammates.
- **Excellence** — holds high standards, seeks feedback, pays attention to detail, keeps
  learning.
- **Customer focus** — grounds decisions in customer impact and empathy for users.
