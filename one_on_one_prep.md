# Prep me for my next 1:1

**1-on-1 preparation** (`one_on_one_prep`)  
Produce a coaching agenda for an upcoming 1-on-1 — key themes, open action items, a conversation framework, and 4–6 questions tailored to the person.

I have a 1:1 coming up with someone on my team. Get me ready for it. I want an agenda, the
open items I've forgotten about, and a few questions worth actually asking — not a generic
template.

Ask me who the 1:1 is with before you start.

## Step 1 — Find the last few 1:1s

**If you can search my meeting notes, calendar, email, or docs**, do it now. Find our last five
1:1s — meetings with their name in the title, or two-person meetings with them. Read the notes
or transcripts.

Tell me what you found before you use it: how many meetings, over what period. If you found
fewer than three, say so — the prep will be thinner and I should know that.

**If you can't search anything**, say so, and ask me to paste in whatever I have: notes from
recent 1:1s, their goals, anything I've written about them. If I have nothing written down, ask me
five focused questions about how the last few conversations went and work from my answers.
Mark anything drawn from my memory rather than a document — memory is worse evidence
than notes and I should be able to see which is which.

## Step 2 — Find the patterns

Across those conversations, pull out:

- **Open commitments** — things one of us said we'd do that probably never got closed. These
  are the most useful thing you can find. Note who owned each and when it was first raised.
- **Recurring themes** — a project, a blocker, a frustration, a goal that keeps resurfacing. If
  something has come up three times and hasn't moved, that's the meeting.
- **Development trajectory** — where are they growing, where are they stuck?
- **Engagement signals** — energised, frustrated, uncertain, checked out? Quote what makes
  you say so.
- **Feedback history** — what I've raised before, and whether anything changed afterwards.
  Feedback given twice with no change is a different conversation from feedback given once.

## Step 3 — Write the prep

Give me this, and nothing else:

### Where they're at

Three or four sentences. Honest, specific, grounded in what you actually read. If the picture is
mixed, say it's mixed. Don't round it up to reassuring.

### Suggested 30-minute agenda

1. **Check-in** (3 min) — one opener tailored to something real from recent context, not "how's
   it going".
2. **Their agenda** (10 min) — theirs first, always. What's on their mind, what's blocking them.
3. **Open items** (7 min) — the specific unresolved things from the list below.
4. **One development topic** (7 min) — the single most useful thing to coach on right now, and
   why that one.
5. **Feedback** (3 min) — only if there's something concrete. If there isn't, say so and drop it.

### Three to five questions worth asking

Real questions about this person's actual situation. Use the GROW shape to make sure you're
not just asking about status:

- **Goal** — what does success look like for them on the thing that keeps coming up?
- **Reality** — what's actually been getting in the way?
- **Options** — what haven't they tried?
- **Will** — what specifically will they do before we next meet?

Pick the ones that fit where this person is right now. Four sharp questions beat ten generic
ones. Skip a stage if it doesn't apply.

### Feedback to give

Only include this if there's something concrete from the evidence. Frame it as
Situation–Behavior–Impact:

- **Situation** — when and where, specifically.
- **Behavior** — what was observably done or said. Not a label. "Answered before others had
  finished their update", not "was dominant".
- **Impact** — what actually followed from it, for the team, the work, or them.

Works for positive feedback too, and most managers underuse it there.

### Open items carried forward

A short table: what was promised, who owns it, when it was first raised, whether it looks done.

## Rules

- **Don't invent.** No commitment, quote, or event that isn't in the evidence or something I told
  you. If the record is thin, a short prep is the honest output.
- **Quote where you can.** "You said you'd unblock the API access on 3 March" is useful. "There
  were some blockers" is not.
- **Don't write the meeting for me.** You're giving me a starting point, not a script. I'll run it.
- **Flag anything I should be careful about** — a wellbeing signal, a retention risk, a pattern
  that suggests they're about to leave. Say it plainly at the top rather than burying it, and be
  clear it's a read from the evidence, not a conclusion.
