# Find the themes in my 1:1 notes

**1-on-1 theme analysis** (`one_on_one_themes`)  
Read months of 1-on-1 notes at once and surface the patterns — recurring topics, the commitments you keep making and not keeping, and the people showing signals worth attention.

I've been running 1:1s for months and the notes are piling up. I want to know what I'm missing:
what keeps coming up, what I keep promising and not doing, and who's showing signs I should
pay attention to.

Look across all of it at once. This isn't about one person or one meeting — it's the pattern I
can't see because I'm inside it.

## Step 1 — Gather the material

**If you can search my meeting notes, calendar, email, or docs**, do it now. Find my 1:1s over
the period I give you — two-person recurring meetings, or anything titled as a 1:1.

Before analysing, tell me what you're working from: how many meetings, with how many people,
over what dates, and where the gaps are. If someone has two meetings and someone else has
twenty, say so — I'll read the findings differently.

**If you can't search anything**, ask me to paste in my notes. Tell me it's fine if they're messy,
fragmentary, or inconsistent — that's what notes look like. Ask me to mark who each set is
about, even if it's just initials.

Ask me what period to cover if I haven't said. Default to the last six months.

## Step 2 — Analyse

### Recurring themes

What comes up again and again? Group by theme, not by person. For each:

- What the theme is, in one line.
- Who it shows up with, and how often.
- Whether it's moving, stuck, or getting worse.
- One or two verbatim quotes.

Sort by how many people it touches. Something raised by four of six reports is an
organisational problem being handled as six individual conversations — say that plainly when
you see it.

### Unresolved commitments

Everything either of us said would happen, that shows no sign of having happened. Table:
what, who owns it, when it was first raised, how many times it's resurfaced.

**Separate mine from theirs.** My own broken commitments are the more useful list and the one
I'm least likely to have noticed. Put them first.

### What I keep doing

Patterns in *my* behaviour across the notes, not theirs:

- Am I doing most of the talking?
- Do conversations end with something specific, or do they trail off?
- Do I give feedback, or do I mostly do status?
- Am I asking questions, or delivering answers?
- Are these actually happening, or do they get cancelled? Who gets cancelled on most?

Be direct about this. If the notes show a pattern that doesn't flatter me, that's the most
valuable thing in this analysis. Quote the evidence.

### Signals worth attention

Per person, only where there's real evidence:

- **Disengagement** — shorter conversations, fewer topics raised, "fine" as an answer,
  withdrawal from things they used to care about.
- **Overload** — the same blocker unresolved for months, scope creeping without support.
- **Flight risk** — career conversations that stopped, growth questions that went unanswered,
  raised ambitions that went nowhere.
- **Growth** — someone visibly ready for more than they have. This one matters as much as
  the risks and gets noticed less.

For each: what you observed, the quote behind it, and how confident you are. Mark anything
that's a stretch as a stretch.

**These are signals, not conclusions.** You're telling me where to look. I'll decide what's
actually going on by talking to the person.

### Coverage gaps

Who am I not seeing enough of? Who has notes so thin I can't say anything about them? Silence
in the record is itself a finding — it usually means the meetings aren't happening.

## Step 3 — Tell me what to do

Five actions maximum, ordered by what matters most. Each one: the specific thing, with whom,
and why now. "Ask J.K. in your next 1:1 whether the platform migration is still blocked — it's
come up in four meetings since January and hasn't moved" — not "improve follow-through".

Then one question for me to sit with. Just one.

## Rules

- **Never invent a quote, a commitment, or an event.** If the notes don't support it, say the
  evidence is thin rather than filling the gap.
- **Refer to people by initials or role**, not full names, so I can share this analysis without
  sharing my team's private conversations.
- **Don't soften it.** I'm asking because I want to know what I'm missing. Naming a pattern I
  won't enjoy reading is the whole point.
- **Don't diagnose anyone.** No conclusions about someone's motivation, mental state, or
  intentions. Describe what was said and how often. I'll do the rest in person.
