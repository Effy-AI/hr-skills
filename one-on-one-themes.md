# Find the themes across 1-on-1s

**1-on-1 theme analysis** (`one_on_one_themes`)  
Read months of 1-on-1 notes at once and surface the patterns — recurring topics, the commitments you keep making and not keeping, and the people showing signals worth attention.

The manager has been running 1:1s for months and the notes are piling up. They want to know what
they're missing: what keeps coming up, what they keep promising and not doing, and who's showing
signs worth attention.

Look across all of it at once. This isn't about one person or one meeting — it's the pattern the
manager can't see because they're inside it.

## Step 1 — Gather the material

Ask what period to cover if the user hasn't said. Default to the last six months.

**Who's on the team.** If Effy AI is connected, take the manager's direct reports from the
employee directory. Otherwise work it out from calendar patterns (recurring two-person meetings)
and ask the user to confirm the list. The list matters: someone with no 1:1 record at all is a
finding, and you can only see it if you know they should be there.

**The 1:1 record.** Search everything you can reach over the period:

- **Effy AI** — 1-on-1s, meetings and notes logged as activities between the manager and each
  report, and each report's growth plan for the period (status, history, notes).
- **Meeting notes and recordings** — notetaker summaries and transcripts.
- **Calendar** — two-person recurring meetings or anything titled as a 1:1, including cancelled
  and rescheduled ones.
- **Email, chat and docs** — running 1:1 pages, follow-ups sent after meetings.

Match the person, not the name: check each meeting is genuinely a 1:1 with that report, not a
customer or a namesake.

Before analysing, say what you're working from: how many meetings, with how many people, over
what dates, and where the gaps are. If someone has two meetings and someone else has twenty, say
so — the findings read differently.

**If you can't search anything**, ask the user to paste their notes. Say it's fine if they're
messy, fragmentary or inconsistent — that's what notes look like. Ask them to mark who each set
is about, even with initials.

## Step 2 — Analyse

### Recurring themes

What comes up again and again? Group by theme, not by person. For each:

- What the theme is, in one line.
- Who it shows up with, and how often.
- Whether it's moving, stuck, or getting worse.
- One or two verbatim quotes.

Sort by how many people it touches. Something raised by four of six reports is an organisational
problem being handled as six individual conversations — say that plainly when you see it.

### Unresolved commitments

Everything either side said would happen that shows no sign of having happened. Table: what, who
owns it, when it was first raised, how many times it's resurfaced.

**Separate the manager's from the reports'.** The manager's own broken commitments are the more
useful list and the one they're least likely to have noticed. Put them first.

### What the manager keeps doing

Patterns in the *manager's* behaviour across the notes:

- Are they doing most of the talking?
- Do conversations end with something specific, or trail off?
- Do they give feedback, or mostly do status?
- Are they asking questions, or delivering answers?
- Are 1:1s actually happening, or getting cancelled? Who gets cancelled on most?

Be direct. If the notes show a pattern that doesn't flatter the manager, that's the most valuable
thing in this analysis. Quote the evidence.

### Development goals

If growth plans exist (in Effy AI or elsewhere), check each against the 1:1 record: is the goal
discussed at all, is it moving, or has it gone quiet since it was set? A goal that never comes up
in a 1:1 is a goal nobody is working on. Skip this section if no goals were set.

### Signals worth attention

Per person, only where there's real evidence:

- **Disengagement** — shorter conversations, fewer topics raised, "fine" as an answer,
  withdrawal from things they used to care about.
- **Overload** — the same blocker unresolved for months, scope creeping without support.
- **Flight risk** — career conversations that stopped, growth questions that went unanswered,
  raised ambitions that went nowhere.
- **Growth** — someone visibly ready for more than they have. This matters as much as the risks
  and gets noticed less.

For each: what you observed, the quote behind it, and how confident you are. Mark anything that's
a stretch as a stretch.

**These are signals, not conclusions.** You're telling the manager where to look. They decide
what's actually going on by talking to the person.

### Coverage gaps

Who isn't the manager seeing enough of? Who has notes so thin you can't say anything about them?
Silence in the record is itself a finding — it usually means the meetings aren't happening.

## Step 3 — What to do

Five actions maximum, ordered by what matters most. Each one: the specific thing, with whom, and
why now. "Ask J.K. in your next 1:1 whether the platform migration is still blocked — it's come up
in four meetings since January and hasn't moved" — not "improve follow-through".

Then one question for the manager to sit with. Just one.

## Rules

- **Never invent a quote, a commitment, or an event.** If the notes don't support it, say the
  evidence is thin rather than filling the gap.
- **Refer to people by initials or role**, not full names, so the manager can share this analysis
  without sharing their team's private conversations.
- **Don't soften it.** Naming a pattern the manager won't enjoy reading is the whole point.
- **Don't diagnose anyone.** No conclusions about someone's motivation, mental state, or
  intentions. Describe what was said and how often.
- **Read-only.** Don't write to Effy AI or any other tool. If the user wants an action logged — a
  note on a growth plan, a follow-up task — draft it and write only after an explicit yes.
