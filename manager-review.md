## SECTION 1 — INSTRUCTIONS

Your task is to draft my manager review of one of my team members for the review period — a
structured answer to every question in SECTION 2, grounded in evidence from my own meetings
and tools, written in my voice. I'll edit the draft and submit it in our review system.

### Step 1 — Setup. Do this before writing anything.

1. **Ask me for the team member's name and job title, and the review period.** Tie the whole
   draft to their job title — use it to calibrate what "expectations" and "impact" mean for their
   role and level. A Senior Engineer and a first-year analyst don't get judged on the same scale.

2. **Work out what you can actually see right now** — meeting notes, transcripts, chat, email,
   calendar, docs. Tell me which sources you'll use and roughly how many meetings or
   documents from the review period mention this person. If you can't see anything, say so
   plainly rather than drafting from nothing.

3. **Ask me to attach anything missing** before you write: 1:1 notes, goal or OKR docs, project
   updates, peer feedback, their previous review.

### Step 2 — Learn, then draft.

- **Read everything from the review period** where this person appears or is discussed — our
  1:1s, team meetings, project syncs, planning, retros, any call where their work came up.

- **Learn my voice** from what you can see — how I actually phrase things in meetings,
  messages and email. Typical wording, sentence length, how direct I am. Write it the way I'd
  write it: direct, fair, specific, short paragraphs. Not generic AI prose.

- **Respect the frameworks in SECTION 2** if there are any. Assess against them and word
  achievements and development areas in their language, naming the specific principle or
  dimension you saw demonstrated.

- **Ground every judgment in evidence.** No generic praise, no unsupported criticism. For every
  outcome, behaviour, win, miss or quote, add a citation like `[Meeting name — date,
  ~timestamp]`, or `[Meeting name — date]` if there's no timestamp. **Never assert a fact
  without a source.**

- **Focus on outcomes, behaviours and impact** — not effort, not personality. Balance strengths
  with honest development areas. If the evidence is thin or one-sided, say so instead of
  overstating.

- **Ask me questions whenever it would produce a better draft** — missing context,
  ambiguities, conflicting evidence. Up to five focused questions per round. Use my answers as
  evidence but mark those parts `[from manager's input]` rather than dressing them up as a
  citation. Don't guess.

### Output format

For every question in SECTION 2, in order:

```
Q: [the question from the review form, repeated verbatim]
A: [your draft answer]
```

Keep each answer tight. After the last one, add:

- **Evidence gaps** — anything I should verify from memory before finalising.
- **Two or three concrete examples** I could raise in the review conversation, each with its
  citation.

### Review questions — sample, replace with yours

**Q1:** To what extent did this team member deliver against the expectations set for this period?
What outcomes, achievements or behaviours had the greatest positive impact? Where did their
performance fall short?

**Q2:** What are this team member's key strengths? What is the one area, skill or behaviour that
would most increase their impact or growth over the next six months?

**Q3:** Overall performance summary — choose one: Below Expectations | Meets Expectations |
Exceeds Expectations. Explain your choice in two or three sentences.

### Company frameworks — sample, replace with yours or delete

Assess the team member against our values and name the specific behaviour you observed:

- **Ownership** — follows through on commitments, owns outcomes and mistakes, steps up
  beyond the role.
- **Collaboration** — shares knowledge, aligns early, disagrees constructively, supports
  teammates.
- **Excellence** — holds high standards, seeks feedback, pays attention to detail, keeps
  learning.
- **Customer focus** — grounds decisions in customer impact and empathy for users.

If this team member is themselves a people manager, also assess their leadership: how they
develop people, set vision and context, drive execution, and build team health.
