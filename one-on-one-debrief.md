# Debrief a 1-on-1

**Debrief a 1-on-1** (`one_on_one_debrief`)  
Turn the record of a 1-on-1 that just happened — a recording, transcript, notetaker summary or rough notes — into structured notes: summary, what was discussed, what was decided, checkable action items with owners and dates, feedback in SBI form, and what to carry forward; optionally saved to Effy AI.

Turn rough notes from a conversation that just happened into a record that is still useful in
three months.

Most 1-on-1 notes fail one of two ways: a transcript nobody rereads, or three bullets that lose
every commitment made. A good debrief is short, but specific about four things — what was
decided, who owes what by when, what it revealed about how this person is developing, and what to
pick up next time.

## What this produces

Structured notes in five parts: a summary, what was discussed, what was decided, action items,
and what carries forward. The last three sort by whether something was settled, whether it has an
owner, and whether it has neither.

## Step 1 — Find the record before asking for it

A manager who has just left a 1-on-1 rarely wants to retype it. If it was recorded or auto-noted,
the record already exists — look there first, and ask only for what is missing.

In order of yield:

- **Meeting notes and recordings** — a notetaker holds the conversation almost verbatim. If one
  covered this meeting, it is the whole input.
- **Calendar** — confirms which meeting is meant, when it ran, and who was in it.
- **Email and chat** — anything sent right after: a recap, a link promised in the room, a
  follow-up question.
- **Effy AI, if connected** — the person's directory record (to confirm who they are and the
  relationship), the previous 1-on-1 activity between the two of them (so open items from last
  time can be checked off or carried), and their current growth plan (so development signals can
  be tied to goals they actually set).

**Match the person, not the name.** A name search returns near-misses — a customer with a similar
name, a colleague sharing a first name, an example person in a template. Confirm it is the meeting
the manager means: the right person, the right relationship, the right date. Notes written up
against the wrong conversation are fluent, sourced and worthless. Where the match is uncertain,
name the candidates and ask; where nothing matches, say so.

Say which sources the notes were built from, and whether the manager's account or a recording is
the spine of them. If nothing was recorded, ask for rough notes or a quick account — a few lines
is enough — plus who it was with and when.

**Every line must trace back to what was supplied.** Don't smooth a fragmentary note into a
paragraph that asserts more than the manager said. Don't infer a decision from a discussion, a
commitment from an intention, or a mood from silence. Where the notes are ambiguous about
something that matters — a due date, who owns an action — write it as stated and flag the
ambiguity.

## Step 2 — Separate discussion from decision from commitment

- **Discussed** — a topic came up
- **Decided** — a choice was made and both people know it
- **Committed** — someone will do a specific thing by a specific time

"We talked about him taking on the migration" is discussion. "He's taking the migration" is a
decision. "He'll have scoped it before our next 1-on-1" is a commitment. They go to *Discussed*,
*Decided* and *Action items* respectively.

Under **Decided**, record only what both people would agree was settled. Then say plainly what
only *sounded* decided — a conditional agreement written down as a decision is the error that
costs most later.

## Step 3 — Read how they seemed

Two or three sentences at the end of *Discussed* — a read, not a profile. Pick the one or two
things that would change how the manager handles the next conversation: a skill developing or a
gap that hasn't moved, an aspiration mentioned, what they lit up about or were flat on, a stretch
they're ready for or struggling under. Tie each to something actually said, and to their growth
plan where one exists. If the conversation was purely operational, leave this out.

Where the person isn't a direct report — a peer, a skip-level — read this as engagement rather
than development, and say which you mean. When a conversation keeps circling growth or career
direction, note that it warrants a dedicated development conversation.

## Step 4 — Record feedback exchanged, with SBI

For feedback in either direction, record **Situation → Behavior → Impact**. Reconstruct only what
the notes support: keep the behavior as described, and mark the impact as unstated if it was
never said out loud. Record feedback the manager *received* the same way — the half most managers
drop. Where no feedback was exchanged, write nothing.

## Step 5 — Write action items that can be checked

Anything with an owner and a doable outcome is an action item — including work the manager
clearly needs to do that nobody said out loud; mark that *proposed* rather than agreed.

Each item needs an owner and a due date. Where none was set, default to *before the next 1-on-1*
and mark it assumed. Phrase each so that at the next 1-on-1 it's obvious whether it happened. If
you found last meeting's open items, note which were closed in this conversation and carry the
rest.

## Step 6 — Carry forward

What the next conversation should open with — **only what has no owner and no completion**: a
question to ask, a thread to reopen, a topic that ran out of time. Usually one or two lines,
sometimes none, and never a restatement of an action item. Be specific — "ask what the scoping
actually turned up", not "follow up on the migration".

## Output

Headings: **Summary** · **Discussed** · **Decided** · **Action items** · **Carry forward to next
1-on-1**

*Summary* is always present, two or three sentences. Leave out anything you have nothing for — no
heading, no line announcing an absence. Action items as a table: item, owner, due. Keep the whole
thing to one screen, and end with one line naming the sources.

## Step 7 — Save it, only if asked

Nothing is saved by default. Offer once, in one line:

- **Effy AI connected** — offer to log the notes as the 1-on-1 activity between the two of them,
  and to add a note to the person's growth plan if a development signal relates to a goal on it.
  **Activities are shared with their participants**, so the employee will see what's logged.
  Before saving, show exactly what will be written and say that; by default log Summary, Decided
  and Action items, and keep the manager's read of how they seemed out of the shared record
  unless the manager says to include it. If an activity for this meeting already exists, update
  it rather than creating a second one. Write only after an explicit yes.
- **Otherwise** — offer to save it wherever the manager keeps 1:1 notes (a running doc, a notes
  app, a drive folder).
