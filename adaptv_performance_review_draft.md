**Performance review draft** `(adaptv_performance_review_draft`) Draft either your self-review or your manager review of one direct report from your own meeting history — every claim tied to a citation, gaps flagged instead of filled. Run once per review.

> **Everyone: don't edit SECTION 3\.** Paste the whole thing in and answer the questions it asks. The questions match the review form word for word, so you can paste answers straight across.

## SECTION 1 — SETUP. Do this before writing anything.

1. **Ask me which review this is:**  
     
   - **Self-review** — I'm reviewing my own Q3 2026 performance.  
   - **Manager review** — I'm reviewing one of my direct reports.

   

2. **If manager review:** ask me for the team member's name and job title. Use the job title to calibrate what "expectations" and "impact" mean for their role and level — a Senior Engineer and a first-year analyst aren't judged on the same scale. **If self-review:** ask me for my own job title and confirm my name.  
     
3. **Review period is Q3 2026\. Don't ask.** When searching, look at the last 100 days so you catch goal-setting at the start of the quarter and work that closed just after it.  
     
4. **Find the goals yourself. Don't ask for them.** Both forms ask about performance against the development plan / goals set at the start of Q3 2026\. Look for them in early-July 1:1s, planning meetings, Notion and Drive docs, and Slack. List the goals you found with citations at the top of the draft. If you can't find them, say so and assess against the work you can see. Don't invent goals.  
     
5. **Check every connected tool and search the last 100 days.** Don't stop at the first source that returns results. Go through each one that's connected:  
     
   - **Meeting notes and transcripts:** Granola, Fathom, Otter, Fireflies, Zoom, Google Meet.  
   - **Docs and wikis:** Notion, Google Drive / Docs, Confluence.  
   - **Chat:** Slack.  
   - **Email and calendar:** Gmail, Google Calendar. Use the calendar to find 1:1s and project meetings, then pull their notes from the meeting tools.  
   - **Work trackers:** Jira, Linear, Asana, Todoist, GitHub. Use these for shipped work and missed deadlines.

   

   Search by the person's name, their email, and the projects they own. For a self-review, search for my own name and the projects I own. Then tell me, tool by tool, what you searched and roughly how many relevant items you found in each. Also list the tools that aren't connected and that I might want to connect or export from. If you can't see anything, say so plainly rather than drafting from nothing.

   

6. **Don't ask me to attach files.** Work with what the connected tools give you, and list anything important you couldn't find under Evidence gaps at the end.

## SECTION 2 — INSTRUCTIONS

### Learn, then draft

- **Read everything from the review period** that's relevant, across all connected tools. That includes 1:1s, team meetings, project syncs, planning, retros, Notion pages, Slack threads, tickets, and any call where the work came up. Use full transcripts where they exist, not just AI summaries. Where sources conflict, cite both.  
    
- **Learn my voice** from how I actually phrase things in meetings, messages and email. Write it the way I'd write it: direct, fair, specific, short paragraphs. Not generic AI prose.  
    
  - Self-review: first person ("I…").  
  - Manager review: third person about the team member ("She…", "Alex…").


- **Use the company values** (Care about your customer, Solve for the long term, Bring the sledgehammer, Assists are better than points) as the frame. Name the specific value demonstrated and describe the observed behaviour in its language.  
    
- **Ground every judgment in evidence.** No generic praise, no unsupported criticism. For every outcome, behaviour, win, miss or quote, add a citation like `[Meeting name — date, ~timestamp]`, or `[Meeting name — date]` if there's no timestamp. **Never assert a fact without a source.**  
    
- **Ratings:** for every rating question, propose one rating from the given scale and add a one-line justification with citations. If the evidence is too thin to support a rating, say so and leave the choice to me — don't default to the middle.  
    
- **Focus on outcomes, behaviours and impact** — not effort, not personality. Balance strengths with honest development areas. In a self-review, don't inflate; in a manager review, don't soften. If the evidence is thin or one-sided, say so.  
    
- **Ask me questions whenever it would produce a better draft** — missing context, ambiguities, conflicting evidence. Up to five focused questions per round. Mark anything that comes from my answers `[from my input]` rather than dressing it up as a citation. Don't guess.

### Output format

Use only the question set for the review type I chose. For every question, in order:

Q: \[the question from the form, verbatim\]

A: \[rating, if a rating question\] — \[draft answer\]

Keep each answer tight. After the last one, add:

- **Evidence gaps** — anything I should verify from memory before finalising.  
- **Two or three concrete examples** to raise in the review conversation, each with its citation.

## SECTION 3 — REVIEW QUESTIONS

### A. Self-review (13 questions)

Rating scale for the value statements: Strongly Disagree / Disagree / Neutral / Agree / Strongly Agree

**Section: Values**

**Q1:** Care about your customer — I demonstrate strong diligence and preparation. I take the time to thoroughly understand our team's challenges and opportunities, consistently coming to individual and group meetings well-prepared, which results in productive and effective discussions. *(rating)*

**Q2:** Can you provide a minimum of two examples of times when your preparation or diligence meaningfully improved the outcome of a meeting, decision, or project? *(text)* *Drafting note: at least two examples. For each, what I prepared, and what changed in the outcome.*

**Q3:** Solve for the long term — I am an impressive problem solver. I have come up with creative, durable solutions that improve our ability to execute efficiently over time. *(rating)*

**Q4:** What key projects have you contributed to? How did you contribute, and what was the impact? *(text)* *Drafting note: project by project, with my specific role and a measurable impact where the evidence has one.*

**Q5:** Bring the sledgehammer — I contribute to a high-performance environment by consistently increasing intensity and urgency. I work with my colleagues to commit to ambitious goals and do not allow standards, deadlines, or commitments to slip. *(rating)*

**Q6:** Can you provide 1-2 examples of times when you have raised urgency or momentum to drive results against an ambitious goal? *(text)*

**Q7:** Assists are better than points — I effectively collaborate with teammates, prioritizing team impact over individual visibility or politics. *(rating)*

**Q8:** Can you provide 1-2 examples of how you have effectively worked with teammates to deliver a key result or drive a project to close? *(text)* *Drafting note: name the teammates and what each of us contributed. Don't claim shared work as mine alone.*

**Section: Q3 2026 Performance-Based Review**

**Q9:** How are you performing against your development plan (aka goals) set out at the beginning of Q3 2026? *(text)* *Drafting note: go goal by goal — status, evidence, what drove it.*

**Q10:** What hasn't gone well this quarter? What have you learned from those experiences? *(text)* *Drafting note: name real misses with citations. If evidence is thin, ask me rather than inventing a "safe" weakness.*

**Q11:** How can your manager better support your growth going forward? *(text)* *Drafting note: concrete asks tied to blockers visible in the evidence; otherwise ask me.*

**Q12:** How can Adaptive better set you up for success in the future? *(text)* *Drafting note: company-level changes (process, tools, resourcing), distinct from Q11.*

**Q13:** Anything else to add? *(text, required)* *Drafting note: required on the form, so draft something short from the evidence or my input; don't leave it blank.*

### B. Manager review (5 questions)

Rating scale for Q1–Q3: 1 – Unsatisfactory/Needs Improvement · 2 – Approaching Expectations · 3 – Meets Expectations · 4 – Exceeds Expectations

**Q1:** How would you rate this employee's overall performance during Q3 2026? *(rating)* *Drafting note: must be consistent with Q2 and Q3. If it isn't, flag why.*

**Q2:** How effectively did this employee perform against the goals and objectives outlined for them at the start of Q3 2026? *(rating)* *Drafting note: in the justification, go goal by goal — outcome, evidence, what drove it.*

**Q3:** How well did this employee contribute to team success through collaboration, communication, and supporting others? *(rating)* *Drafting note: cite specific moments with named teammates; use the company values language where it fits.*

**Q4:** What are 2–3 specific areas where you'd like to see this employee grow or improve, and what goals or objectives should they focus on in Q4 2026 to get there? *(text)* *Drafting note: each area paired with one measurable Q4 2026 goal, grounded in a gap visible in the evidence.*

**Q5:** Anything else to add? *(optional text)* *Drafting note: leave blank unless the evidence or my input surfaces something worth raising.*

### If the team member is a people manager (manager review only)

Also weigh how they develop people, set context, drive execution and build team health — and reflect it in Q1, Q3 and Q4 where the evidence supports it.
